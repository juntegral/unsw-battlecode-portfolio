How our team turns ideas into a better bot

We work in three ways at once. In the first, Zhe brings ideas from watching games and makes the final call. In the second, an AI works through the ideas we already have. In the third, an AI studies our past games and comes up with ideas on its own. All three share one idea board, one test setup and one bot, so nothing drifts apart.

The three workflows in detail

The examples are made up to show how each workflow runs. Their numbers are invented.

① Zhe's workflow

Used forIdeas that come from watching how strong teams play.

  1. Zhe watches a top game and picks one moment.
  2. Zhe writes a card: what our bot should do there, why it holds up against the opponent's next moves, and what would show it is wrong.
  3. The AI builds the change behind a switch that starts off, and tests it in hundreds of local games.
  4. The AI lists the key moments where the new behaviour kicked in (game number + round).
  5. Zhe watches those moments and decides: keep it, adjust and retry, or drop it.

ExampleZhe sees the other team's small dragons feeding their leader at round 320 while ours wander → card → the AI builds “feed the leader from round 300” → Zhe watches 5 moments → keeps it, but adds “only if no enemy is within 6 steps”.

AI useLow. The AI only builds, tests and lists moments.

② Board loop

Used forIdeas we already have: QWQ's list of the bot's biggest problems, hypotheses from our previous teams, and anything waiting on the board.

  1. Someone marks an idea on the board as ready.
  2. Every hour, the AI takes the next ready idea.
  3. It builds the change behind a switch and tests it in hundreds of local games.
  4. It posts the results on the idea's page, and the person who owns that part of the bot decides.

ExampleIdea “the queen stops splitting when enemies are near” → tested on the 22 official maps → 12 games turned into wins and 5 into losses → QWQ, who owns the queen part, keeps it.

AI useMedium. One idea per hour.

③ Full-AI workflow

Used forFinding problems nobody has noticed yet. This is where we spend AI on purpose.

  1. The AI reads all our past ladder games and counts where we lose dragons and games.
  2. It turns the biggest patterns into ideas and writes them on the board.
  3. It builds and tests each idea.
  4. It checks the replays itself: did the new behaviour switch on, and did it help?
  5. A person gives a quick OK before anything goes into the bot.

ExampleThe AI finds that 41% of our early deaths on big maps happen in dead-end pockets → idea “don't enter a pocket smaller than our own length” → tests → replays show pocket deaths fall from 50 to 12 per game → proposed for the bot.

AI useHigh, on purpose. Reading many games and replays is where the AI is most useful.

Words used on this page

If a word is unfamiliar, this is what we mean by it.

AI loop
An AI that repeats the same job on a schedule, for example every hour, without anyone typing each command.
Idea board / issue
A list of ideas on GitHub. Each idea is one issue, and all results are posted as comments under it.
Switch (toggle)
New behaviour is added to the code switched off. The bot does not change until we decide to switch it on.
Comparison bot (baseline)
The current bot. Every test compares a change against it.
Local games
Games we play on our own computers: the bot with the switch on against the bot with it off, on the same maps and random seeds.
Replay
A recording of a game that we can watch move by move.
Key moment
The exact game and round where a new behaviour switched on. The bot leaves a short note in the replay so the AI can find it.
Ladder
The online server where our uploaded bot plays other teams.
main
The one official copy of the bot's code. Every kept change ends up here.
Tokens
The unit AI usage is measured in. Each plan has a weekly limit.

How the three workflows stay in sync

  • One copy of the code. Every kept change goes into main as a switch. Nobody keeps a separate version of the bot for long.
  • Results say which version they were measured on. When main changes, older results are marked out of date and the comparison bot is measured again.
  • The AI starts fresh every time. Each run reads only the board and main, so if a computer goes to sleep, nothing is lost.
  • A "working on it" label. loop:running stops two AI runs from taking the same idea.
  • Write it on the board. Discord is for chatting. Anything we will need later goes in a comment on the idea.

Where the AI usage goes

AI usage is counted in tokens. It grows with how many steps the AI takes times how much it has to re-read at each step. In our old setup, more than 97% of all tokens were the AI re-reading what it had already seen, and less than 0.1% was text it actually wrote. So scripts do the counting, and the AI reads short summaries.
