# WeHaveQuizzes read backwards: rank-1's rams, a queen fed from round 200, and a swarm that folds into a few long dragons after round 300 (team 1119; 180 online games of 8 Oct, 597 rank-1 ladder games; written 8 Oct 2026)

1. **Finding.** WeHaveQuizzes (WHQ) took 72 of the 90 games, and they split three ways by what decided them: 25 eliminations (all on 1200 cells or fewer, behind on count for good from round 20), 26 on queen length (their queen alive in all 26 and rammed in none, fed from round 200 to a median 78; ours dead in 22), 21 on longest dragon (17 above 1200 cells; 64 against 25). Three behaviours carry that. After round 300 its dragons stop beside a team mate twice their length and the swarm folds from 53 dragons into 13 (97 stops a game above 1200 cells, mean length 4.5). Its queen walks inside the swarm and is fed first. A 3-long pays a segment to ram a head two tiles off (45% of its 42.5 rams a game), which is rank-1's attack nearly number for number. Big Bobs Bots (BBB) rams as much and folds later and weaker; rank-1 rams the same and folds half as hard.
2. **Change.** One card, and it is the one diag-longest reached in parallel from our own side (endgame rally; count it once): after the feed round a short dragon walks to the leader it knows from any distance (the rally branch is in the code and has never been on). In the 9 bundle-build losses on longest dragon we ended with 38 dragons, 200 total length and a longest of 30 against 11, 212 and 60, and our total was at least twice their longest in 8 of the 9. After round 400, 82% of our dragon-turns above 1200 cells start 7 or more tiles from any mate twice their length, and nothing sends them to one.
3. **How sure.** Sure of the anatomy: every turn of all 777 games through my own state machine, final state and winner equal to the replay's own in every game; results, deciding rule and queen fates equal online/agg7.md count for count (5/7/6 and 16/16/8 wins; 14/13/0/18 on main against WHQ; 49 queen rams on main), stops counted alike by three of my scripts, and the 82% of the card recounted by a second script at round starts (81.8%; theirs 71.4% against 71.0%). Sure the dying rule is not the gap: once beside such a mate ours stop on 21% of turns, theirs on 16-18%. Two scans by two analysts reached the same switch. About one in three that it changes results: it reaches 28 of 122 losses, and the walk has never been played.
