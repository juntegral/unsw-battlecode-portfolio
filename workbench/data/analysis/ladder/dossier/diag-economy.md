# Of the 25 pearls, 20 are paid back in deaths; what is left to take is the pearl in a small room (team 1119; 500 main ladder games, 597 of rank-1's; written 8 Oct 2026)

1. **Where the 25 pearls per 1,000 turns go.** By origin: 12.7 are natural pearls (as many are born per turn in its games as in ours, 85.2 against 86.8 per 1,000 of the side's turns; it eats 55.0% of them and we 42.4%), 11.9 are its own dead eaten again, 0.7 the other side's dead. Rank-1 also loses 20.3 more length per 1,000 turns in deaths, so the net gap is 4.9 (13.6 against 8.6). By situation: every pearl is eaten on a turn that starts with one beside the head; 6.9 of the gap is pearls on cramped tiles (rank-1 starts a turn cornered beside a pearl twice as often, 19.1 against 10.1 per 1,000 turns), 4.1 is what our short dragons have in view (nothing at all on 24.3% of all turns against 17.4%), 2.9 the leader zone before round 300, 4.7 short dragons after round 300, 4.2 dragons of 4 or longer, 2.4 the rest.
2. **The change.** One switch, CRAMPED_EAT (default off): a step that eats a pearl and is not trapped (at least length + 2 tiles behind it) no longer pays the tight-room penalty and the no-way-to-own-tail penalty.
3. **How sure.** Sure of the account: every turn of all 1,097 games, turns and pearls equal to the earlier index in every game, the total reproduces 58.82 / 84.06 and the parts sum to 25.24. Fairly sure of what the card rests on: ours declines a pearl in a small room that rank-1 takes, on 14 of 14 layouts by the bot's own room count (48 and 54% against 89 and 81%), the lines that price the step say so, and a recount by other code agrees in direction with half the gap (64% against 78%). Not sure the switch moves the rate (three terms price the step and it waives two), and it is small by construction: about 3 pearl steps a game, a fifth of the net gap on paper, 1 of the 25. No single switch closes the 25, and most of it should not be closed.
