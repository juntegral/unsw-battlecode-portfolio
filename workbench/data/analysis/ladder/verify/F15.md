# F15: holds

**Verdict: holds.** Every count reproduces exactly. Of our 5895 no-action deaths on main, 0.928 happen within 8
tiles of one of our own dragons at least twice as long (0.872 within 3), our side eats 0.804 of the 8565 pearls
they drop within 15 rounds and the opponent 0.033; v31 is the same (0.930, 0.794, 0.035) and so is each rating
band. It is a rule and not a crash: all 16221 are plain no-action replies in the dragon's own turn, none happens
before raw round 201 in any of 620 games, every single one has one of our heads within 3 tiles and not one has
an enemy head within 3. Two things to keep next to the word "working". The 80 percent is of pearls, and a body
drops one pearl per two segments: we get back 6883 of 15455 length given up, 0.445. And the split is not an
edge over the opponents: their own no-action deaths are eaten by their own side at 0.839 and by us at 0.028.

## Checks

- **All of ours are no action.** Main 5895 of 5895 and v31 10326 of 10326 are the no-action kind, sent in the
  dragon's own turn; no split, no unpaid sprint, no timeout. The queen is never one of them on main; on v31 she
  is twice.
- **Chance.** Bootstrap over games, 95 percent, main: within 3 0.848 to 0.895, within 8 0.909 to 0.945, eaten by
  us 0.789 to 0.817, eaten by them 0.026 to 0.041. v31: 0.852 to 0.886, 0.918 to 0.940, 0.783 to 0.804, 0.029
  to 0.043.
- **One opponent, map, evening, three games.** 138 of 210 main games have at least one (170 reached r200),
  median 28 per game, at most 150. Without the three games with most: 0.928, 0.802, 0.034. Across the 22
  opponents with 50 or more such deaths: within 8 from 0.836 to 0.986, eaten by us 0.738 to 0.844, by them
  0.009 to 0.122. Across the 13 layouts with 50 or more: within 8 from 0.732 (Default) to 0.989, eaten by us
  from 0.654 (weakhold) to 1.000 (Trophy, 56 deaths), by them up to 0.085 (Australia). v31: weakhold again
  lowest at 0.622, by them up to 0.131 on Islands. The two full evenings: 0.804 and 0.810. So "nine in ten"
  holds on 9 of these 13 layouts on main and falls short on Default (0.73 main, 0.78 v31), weakhold (0.84,
  0.81), Schooltime #1 (0.87 main) and Maze (0.89, 0.88), and "under 5 percent to
  them" fails on Around UNSW (0.061), Queen Of Spades (0.064) and Australia (0.085) on main.
- **Is "within 8 of a big dragon" automatic?** Partly a low bar: the dying dragon is 2 long in 3994 deaths and 3
  long in 1494, so "twice as long" means 4 or 6. But it is not automatic: on average 7.4 of our 40.9 other
  living dragons qualify, and yet one of them is within 3 tiles in 87 percent of the deaths (distance 1: 195, 2:
  1659, 3: 3289, 4 to 8: 329, further: 365, none alive: 58). The 4-to-8 band adds little; this is a within-3
  fact.
- **Does the big dragon matter for the eating?** Less than the wording suggests. When the big dragon is within 3
  we eat 0.814 of the drop; when it is beyond 8 or absent we still eat 0.728 (604 pearls) and they eat 0.083.
  Some dragon of ours is always within 3. I did not record which dragon eats, so "fed to the big dragon" is not
  checked here, only "fed to our side, next to a big dragon".
- **Same measure for the opponents.** Their reason-4 deaths in our main games: 8609, of which 7641 no action,
  851 illegal split, 90 unpaid sprint, 27 timeout. Within 3 of an own dragon twice as long 0.443, within 8
  0.801, eaten by their own side within 15 rounds 0.839, by us 0.028; 1977 of them before r200, the first at
  r0. In our v31 games: 20277 deaths, within 8 0.681, own side 0.839, us 0.024. So recovering four fifths of
  such a drop is what both sides do. What is ours alone is the tight pattern: nothing before r201, always
  beside one of ours, never beside one of theirs.
- **When.** Main by 50-round bin from r200: 728, 542, 642, 654, 2122, 1207. 56 percent come after r400.
- **What it costs.** 15455 length given up, 8565 pearls dropped, 6883 back within 15 rounds and 953 later (to
  either side). The total on the board goes down by about half of what is given up; whether that is worth it
  depends on where the length ends up, which this claim does not measure.
